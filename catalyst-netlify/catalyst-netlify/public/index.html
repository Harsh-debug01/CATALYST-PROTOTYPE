Catalyst

A research and learning engine that reconciles what you've written against what you've read — then gets out of your way.

scroll
01 — The problem
Notes drift from sources. Nobody notices until the exam.

You read a paper, a chapter, a lecture. You write your own version of it. Weeks later the two have quietly diverged — a dropped condition, a misremembered mechanism, a step skipped for being "obvious." Catalyst's reconciliation engine compares your notes against the source material continuously, classifies each gap by importance, and folds the result into a learner profile that sharpens every time you write.

02 — The stance
Every other AI browser wants to act for you. Catalyst wants to think with you.

Comet and Atlas browse and click on your behalf. Catalyst never does — it reads, highlights, compares, and explains, but every save, every note, every quiz question is something you asked for by name.

Agentic browsers

  • Take actions autonomously
  • Optimize for task completion
  • You review after the fact

Catalyst

  • Surfaces, compares, explains
  • Optimizes for understanding
  • You decide before anything is saved
03 — The loop
Search. Read. Note. Reconcile. Recall.

Search opens inside Catalyst, not in a new tab. The AI highlights what matters and saves it to your library on command. Your notes, your saved clippings, your PDFs and images all live in one place — ready to become a summary, a mind map, or a quiz whenever you need one.

04 — How it's built
A thin client, a small proxy, and Claude doing the real work.

The browser never holds an API key and never talks to Anthropic directly. Every AI call — search, chat, summarize, reconcile, mind map, quiz — goes through one small backend proxy that holds the real credential and forwards the request.

Deliberate choices

  • No API key ever reaches the browser
  • Every AI action is triggered by the user, never automatic
  • Session-only storage — nothing persists without an explicit export

Why it matters

  • Same architecture works locally or deployed publicly, unchanged
  • The model and its credentials are swappable server-side with zero frontend changes
  • A stolen browser tab can't leak an API key that was never in it
